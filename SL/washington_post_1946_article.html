THURSDAY, MARCH 21, 1946
Page A-7

Displaced Persons From Eastern Front Receive Treatment in U.S. Facilities

German Refugees Among Those Assisted by Medical Aid Program

By ROBERT J. HARRISON
Staff Correspondent

A small number of German civilians displaced by the Eastern Front campaigns are receiving specialized medical treatment at American facilities, according to sources familiar with the War Department's humanitarian assistance programs.

The patients, primarily civilians caught in the Leningrad siege and subsequent Soviet advance, were evacuated through Sweden in 1946 and are being treated for what military medical officers describe as "severe psychological trauma and unusual physiological conditions."

Among the facilities providing care is the Frankfurt Klinik für Neuropsychiatrie in the American occupation zone, which has been treating both German nationals and displaced persons since late 1945. Dr. Klaus Reinhardt, the clinic's director, declined to provide specific details about patient conditions, citing medical confidentiality.

"Many of these individuals experienced conditions during the siege that are difficult to comprehend," Dr. Reinhardt said in a brief statement. "Our primary concern is their recovery and eventual reintegration."

The War Department emphasized that the assistance program is limited in scope and focuses on humanitarian concerns rather than political considerations. Officials noted that similar programs exist for displaced persons from other war-torn regions.

Congressional critics, including Rep. John Rankin (D-Miss.), have questioned the appropriateness of providing American resources to former enemy nationals while American servicemen await demobilization. The War Department maintains that the program aligns with international humanitarian principles established at the Geneva Convention.

Approximately 40-60 individuals are estimated to be receiving such specialized care, though exact numbers remain classified for security reasons. The program is expected to continue through 1947.
