DATE: September 1, 1940
TO: Director, FBI
FROM: SAC, Washington Field Office
RE: CRASH OF PENNSYLVANIA CENTRAL AIRLINES FLIGHT 19
AUGUST 31, 1940 - LOVETTSVILLE, VIRGINIA
INVESTIGATIVE MATTER

SUMMARY

Investigation into crash of Pennsylvania Central Airlines Flight 19 initiated at request of XXXXXXXXXXXXXXX. Flight departed Washington National Airport at approximately 4:23 PM en route to Pittsburgh and Detroit. Aircraft crashed approximately 4:55 PM in mountainous terrain near Lovettsville, Loudoun County, Virginia.

All twenty-five (25) persons aboard killed, including United States Senator ERNEST LUNDEEN of Minnesota.

INITIAL FINDINGS

Agents arrived at crash site at approximately 8:45 PM August 31, 1940. Scene secured by Loudoun County Sheriff's Office. Weather conditions at time of crash: thunderstorms, heavy rain, poor visibility.

Wreckage scattered over approximately XXXXX acres. Fuselage largely intact but severely damaged by fire. No survivors.

Senator Lundeen's briefcase was NOT recovered at crash site.

Witness HARRIET JOHNSON (Senator's secretary, not aboard flight) interviewed September 1, 1940. States Senator carried brown leather briefcase when departing office. Briefcase described as containing XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX.

WITNESS STATEMENTS - LOVETTSVILLE AREA

The following individuals were interviewed regarding unusual circumstances surrounding the crash:

WALTER BISHOP (farmer, property owner where aircraft crashed)

States he observed aircraft descending through storm clouds at approximately 4:50 PM. Reports aircraft appeared to be "glowing with blue light" before impact. ← unusual

NOTE: Weather conditions may explain visual anomaly.

ELEANOR MARSH (resident, Lovettsville)

States she heard "humming sound" distinct from aircraft engine approximately five (5) minutes before crash. Described sound as "like nothing I ever heard before - made my teeth hurt."

JAMES WHITMAN (postal carrier)

Reports observing "three men" near crash site approximately 2:00 AM September 1, 1940, before official recovery teams arrived. States men were "digging in the wreckage" and departed in dark sedan when approached. Vehicle registration: XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX
