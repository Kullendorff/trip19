Special Note — "ESCAPED" Category

Files F.07, F.16, and F.23 — Detailed Listing
Addendum to p. 3, drafted 10. X. 1952

The following three files were identified during the regular inventory as lacking a valid transfer protocol. A subsequent reconstruction of events produced the following picture:

File F.07

Clinic ID:K-07 (Kirov, Moscow)
Sex / Age:male / at intake 1942: ca. 6 years
Arrival Güldendorf:6 March 1952
Last sighting:Güldendorf, 14 March 1952 — morning roll call
Reported missing:15 March 1952, 06:30
Circumstances:Left Dormitory C between 02:00 and 05:00. No sign of force. Door was opened from the inside. Patient was not wearing outer clothing.
Presumed whereabouts:West Berlin (American sector) — basis: anonymous tip received by the People's Police Frankfurt/Oder on 3 IV 1952
Search:not initiated — directive from Department IV, Gen. Marshall

File F.16

Clinic ID:K-16 (Kirov, Moscow)
Sex / Age:male / at intake 1942: ca. 4 years
Arrival Güldendorf:11 March 1952
Last sighting:Güldendorf, 20 July 1952 — evening roll call
Reported missing:21 July 1952, 07:00
Circumstances:During maintenance work on the side wing. Patient was assigned to kitchen duty. Contact with an East Berlin civilian (name unverified) on 18 VII known.
Presumed whereabouts:East Berlin — contact lost 07/1952. Rumours suggest he was taken in by a private household.
Search:initiated, suspended 14 IX 1952 — without result

File F.23 — Special Case

Clinic ID:K-23 (Kirov, Moscow)
Sex / Age:[missing from transport manifest]
Arrival Güldendorf:— per manifest: 14. III. 1952 —
Actual arrival:unverifiable. Folder was empty on arrival.
Circumstances:Folder F.23 was located on 14 May 1952 on archive shelf B-IV. It was empty. No patient, no documents, no X-rays. The envelope was undamaged.
Outgoing protocol:cannot be located
Transport order:cannot be located
Presumed whereabouts:unknown. Neither USSR nor GDR authorities acknowledge jurisdiction.
Search:not possible — no name, no photograph, no X-ray on file

Handwritten Note

Annex to Special Note F.23 — p. 14 of the Inventory
Author: Dr. med. H. Schäfer  ·  3 November 1952

Frankfurt (Oder), 3 November 1952.

To the esteemed leadership of Department IV —

I feel it is my duty to add a personal note at the close of this inventory — one which I could not include in the official record.

File F.23 was not transferred by us. The folder was empty on 14 May. There was no outgoing protocol. No transport order. Neither the Ministry in Moscow nor our own department has any knowledge of who removed the file or the patient.

I personally inspected archive shelf B-IV. The seal was intact. The envelope showed no signs of forced entry. Only the inner document was missing — together with the X-ray, the case history, and the photograph.

This means: someone with key authority removed the material between 14 March and 14 May 1952. There are only four persons at Güldendorf with access to B-IV. I am one of them. The other three have been questioned — without result.

I hereby state that, in my judgement, the whereabouts of patient F.23 lie beyond the reach of the USSR and the GDR. Whether in West Berlin, in the British or American zone — or further afield — I cannot say.

I request that the leadership of Department IV preserve this note and present it should inquiries be made by higher authorities. I will undertake no further investigation.

— H. Sch.

H. Schäfer
Dr. med. Helmut Schäfer, Senior Physician
Güldendorf Clinic, Dept. of Special Research
Frankfurt an der Oder, 3 November 1952
